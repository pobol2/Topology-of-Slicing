\section{Framework and interruption guarantees}
\label{sec:cms}

\subsection{Spaces, paths, and the map chain}
\label{sec:cms-spaces}

Let $\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^3$ be compact, with target deposition measure $\mu_\Omega$. All measures are probability measures, and $f_\#\mu(E)=\mu(f^{-1}(E))$ is the pushforward of a measure $\mu$ by a measurable map $f$. The target measure may be volumetric or live on lower-dimensional sets such as layers. Let $\Qs$ be the configuration space of the machine \cite{lavalle2006}, with the tool-position forward-kinematics map
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:cms-forward-kinematics}
  \FK:\Qs\to\mathbb{R}^3,\qquad {\vect{x}}=\FK\circ {\vect{q}}.
\end{equation}
The static admissible set $\Qfree\subseteq\Qs$ encodes joint limits, a prescribed singularity margin, and avoidance of fixed obstacles.

\paragraph{Deposition time.}
Time $t\in[0,1]$ is normalised deposition time, and $\lambda^1$ is the uniform probability measure on $[0,1]$. For a space $M$, $\Paths{M}$ denotes the paths $[0,1]\to M$ that are continuous on the pieces of a finite partition of $[0,1]$, with one-sided limits at the breakpoints; it contains the continuous paths. Physical execution crosses each discontinuity with a non-deposition travel move, which takes machine time but no deposition time. The traversal ${\vect{\upxi}}$, tool path ${\vect{x}}$, and joint path ${\vect{q}}$ share the time parameter.

\begin{definition}[Map chain]
  \label{def:cms}
  For a prescribed target $(\Omega,\mu_\Omega)$, a geometry-first map chain is a tuple
  $(\Xs,\mu_{\Xs},{\vect{\upxi}},\Phi,\Psi_{\mathrm{g}})$, where:
  \begin{itemize}
    \item $\Xs$ is a compact manifold with boundary, or a finite disjoint union of such manifolds, of dimension at most three;
    \item $\mu_{\Xs}$ is a probability measure on $\Xs$;
    \item ${\vect{\upxi}}\in\Paths{\Xs}$ is the traversal, satisfying ${\vect{\upxi}}_\#\lambda^1=\mu_{\Xs}$;
    \item $\Phi:\Xs\to\Omega$ is a measurable geometry map, satisfying $\Phi_\#\mu_{\Xs}=\mu_\Omega$;
    \item $\Psi_{\mathrm{g}}$ is a configuration lift, taking the tool paths it admits to joint paths in $\Paths{\Qs}$ and satisfying
          \begin{equation}
            \label{eq:cms-lift}
            \FK\circ\Psi_{\mathrm{g}}[{\vect{x}}]={\vect{x}}\qquad\text{for every admitted }{\vect{x}}.
          \end{equation}
  \end{itemize}
\end{definition}
The lift applies only when ${\vect{x}}$ has finitely many discontinuities, which is not automatic for a measurable $\Phi$ (Subsection~\ref{sec:vsfc-ot}); applicability of the lift and static admissibility therefore require separate certificates. With both pushforward conditions, \eqref{eq:main-measure} gives the tool path the occupation measure $\mu_\Omega$. The process operator $\Theta$, which maps a joint path to a manufactured set for a fixed process and protocol, is left abstract. For a finite planar raster, taking $\mu_{\Xs}$ to be the normalised occupation measure of the zigzag centreline, rather than the area measure on each slice, makes the chain exact. Definition~\ref{def:cms} and the certificates below concern the geometry-first order; the kinematics-first chain is \eqref{eq:kinematicsfirst}.

The lift acts on whole paths because, for an arm with more joints than tool coordinates, the fibre $\FK^{-1}({\vect{y}})$ over a regular tool position ${\vect{y}}\in\mathbb R^3$ has positive dimension, the number of joints minus three \cite{burdick1989}: no configuration in it is canonical, and singularities can prevent a globally continuous inverse branch \cite{gottlieb1988robots,pavesic2017topologist,zhang2021singularity}. A lift is therefore selected along the whole path \cite{kazerounian1988global,chalvin2020layer,yang2020coverage}.

\subsection{Certificates that compose}
\label{sec:cms-composition}

A \emph{certificate} is a property of one stage alone, on a specified domain. Five kinds are used:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{Coverage:} a pushforward identity, or a measure error bounded in $W_\infty$.
  \item \emph{Regularity:} a modulus of continuity $\omega_f(\tau)=\sup_{\dist(e,e')\leq\tau}\dist(f(e),f(e'))$ of a stage $f$, where $\dist$ is the metric of each space and $e,e'$ range over the domain of $f$.
  \item \emph{Discontinuity:} the set $\Sigma(f)$ of discontinuity points, or a certified set containing it.
  \item \emph{Containment:} a region containing the image of the stage.
  \item \emph{Stability:} a uniform implementation error $\|\hat f-f\|_\infty\leq\varepsilon_f$, measured in the target space of the stage.
\end{itemize}
For probability measures on a compact metric space, $W_\infty$ \cite{champion2008infinity} is the smallest distance within which some coupling moves all of the mass. For paths with discontinuities, regularity certificates hold on each continuous piece.

\begin{proposition}[Composition rules]
  \label{prop:composition}
  Let $f\colon \mathcal{A}\to \mathcal{B}$ and $g\colon \mathcal{B}\to \mathcal{C}$ be measurable stages between compact metric spaces, $\mu_{\mathcal{A}}$ a probability measure on $\mathcal{A}$, and $\mu_{\mathcal{B}}$ and $\mu_{\mathcal{C}}$ target probability measures on $\mathcal{B}$ and $\mathcal{C}$.
  \begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
    \item \emph{Coverage.} $W_\infty\bigl((g\circ f)_\#\mu_{\mathcal{A}},\mu_{\mathcal{C}}\bigr)\leq\omega_g\bigl(W_\infty(f_\#\mu_{\mathcal{A}},\mu_{\mathcal{B}})\bigr)+W_\infty(g_\#\mu_{\mathcal{B}},\mu_{\mathcal{C}})$. In particular, exact pushforward certificates compose exactly.
    \item \emph{Regularity.} $\omega_{g\circ f}\leq\omega_g\circ\omega_f$.
    \item \emph{Discontinuity.} $\Sigma(g\circ f)\subseteq\Sigma(f)\cup f^{-1}\bigl(\Sigma(g)\bigr)$.
    \item \emph{Containment.} If $f(\mathcal{A})\subseteq \mathcal{B}'$ and $g(\mathcal{B}')\subseteq \mathcal{C}'$, then $(g\circ f)(\mathcal{A})\subseteq \mathcal{C}'$.
    \item \emph{Stability.} If implementations $\hat f,\hat g$ satisfy $\lVert\hat f-f\rVert_\infty\leq\varepsilon_f$ on $\mathcal{A}$ and $\lVert\hat g-g\rVert_\infty\leq\varepsilon_g$ on $\hat f(\mathcal{A})$, with $\omega_g$ taken on $f(\mathcal{A})\cup\hat f(\mathcal{A})$, then $\lVert\hat g\circ\hat f-g\circ f\rVert_\infty\leq\varepsilon_g+\omega_g(\varepsilon_f)$.
  \end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
  For (i), an optimal $W_\infty$ coupling of $f_\#\mu_{\mathcal{A}}$ and $\mu_{\mathcal{B}}$ exists on compact spaces \cite{champion2008infinity}; push it forward by $g\times g$ and apply the triangle inequality for $W_\infty$. Items (ii)--(iv) are immediate from the definitions, and (v) follows from $\lVert\hat g\circ\hat f-g\circ\hat f\rVert_\infty+\lVert g\circ\hat f-g\circ f\rVert_\infty$.
\end{proof}
Rule (iii) is an upper bound; Proposition~\ref{prop:topology-transitions} gives the matching lower bound for disconnected sources. Rule (ii) multiplies H\"older exponents.

\begin{corollary}[$W_\infty$ gives a covering radius]
  \label{cor:cms-covering-radius}
  For every probability measure $\mu$ on $\Omega$, every point of $\supp\mu_\Omega$ lies within distance $W_\infty(\mu,\mu_\Omega)$ of $\supp\mu$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
  An optimal coupling matches almost every target point to a point of $\supp\mu$ within distance $W_\infty(\mu,\mu_\Omega)$. Distance to this closed set is continuous, and every neighbourhood of a point of $\supp\mu_\Omega$ has positive mass, so the bound extends to all of $\supp\mu_\Omega$.
\end{proof}
With $\supp\mu_\Omega=\Omega$, one coupling bound therefore controls both the distribution of deposition time and the covering radius of the path.

Because $\Psi_{\mathrm{g}}$ acts on paths, its certificates take a different form: a nondecreasing transfer function $T_\Psi$, such that an input path with modulus at most $\omega$ lifts to a joint path with modulus at most $T_\Psi(\omega)$; a set $B_\Psi$ of branch-switch times, with $\Sigma({\vect{q}})\subseteq\Sigma({\vect{x}})\cup B_\Psi$; and a region ${\Omega_{\mathrm{lift}}}\subseteq\Omega$ whose admitted paths lift into $\Qfree$. A pointwise section with Lipschitz constant $L_\Psi$ has $T_\Psi(\omega)=L_\Psi\,\omega$. Table~\ref{tab:cms-guarantees} assembles the certificates; replacing a stage changes only its column.

\subsection{Transitions of disconnected sources}
\label{sec:topology}

Suppose the source is a finite disjoint union of compact connected components,
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:topology-source}
  \Xs=\bigsqcup_{j=1}^{N}\Xs_j.
\end{equation}
Planar layers give at least one component per layer; curved layers \cite{etienne2019curvi,zhang2022s3}, separate surfaces \cite{zhong2023acap}, and parameterisation patches \cite{ezair2018volumetric} give others. The components are those of the source, not of the part: the images $\Phi(\Xs_j)$ can touch or overlap in $\Omega$. Four kinds of interruption must be kept apart.

\begin{definition}[Interruptions]
  \label{def:topology-interruptions}
  \leavevmode
  \begin{itemize}
    \item A \emph{source interruption} is a discontinuity of the traversal ${\vect{\upxi}}$; a \emph{transition} is a source interruption at which ${\vect{\upxi}}$ passes from one component to another.
    \item A \emph{tool-path interruption} is a discontinuity of ${\vect{x}}=\Phi\circ{\vect{\upxi}}$.
    \item A \emph{machine interruption} is a discontinuity of the joint path ${\vect{q}}=\Psi_{\mathrm{g}}[{\vect{x}}]$.
    \item A \emph{deposition interruption} is a stop of the material flow during physical execution.
  \end{itemize}
\end{definition}
The first three occur in deposition time; the last occurs in machine time, which also includes travel moves.

\begin{proposition}[Source-component transitions]
  \label{prop:topology-transitions}
  Let $\Xs$ be as in \eqref{eq:topology-source}, let ${\vect{\upxi}}\in\Paths{\Xs}$ satisfy ${\vect{\upxi}}_\#\lambda^1=\mu_{\Xs}$, and suppose $\mu_{\Xs}(\Xs_j)>0$ for every $j$.
  \begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
    \item The traversal ${\vect{\upxi}}$ has at least $N-1$ transitions, whatever the order in which it visits the components.
    \item Suppose $\Phi$ is continuous on each component. At a transition at time $t$, the tool path ${\vect{x}}=\Phi\circ{\vect{\upxi}}$ has the one-sided limits ${\vect{x}}(t^\pm)=\Phi({\vect{\upxi}}(t^\pm))$, and it is discontinuous at $t$ unless
          \begin{equation}
            \label{eq:topology-identify}
            \Phi\bigl({\vect{\upxi}}(t^-)\bigr)=\Phi\bigl({\vect{\upxi}}(t^+)\bigr).
          \end{equation}
  \end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
  (i) Take a finite partition of $[0,1]$ on whose open pieces ${\vect{\upxi}}$ is continuous. The image of each piece is connected, so it lies in one component; label the piece by that component. Each component has positive mass, so by ${\vect{\upxi}}_\#\lambda^1=\mu_{\Xs}$ it is visited for a positive time, and its label appears on at least one piece. Merge consecutive pieces with the same label. The resulting sequence contains all $N$ labels, with adjacent labels distinct, so it changes label at least $N-1$ times, and each change is a transition. (ii) ${\vect{\upxi}}(t')\to{\vect{\upxi}}(t^-)$ within one compact component as $t'\to t^-$, and $\Phi$ is continuous there; the same holds from the right.
\end{proof}
Condition~\eqref{eq:topology-identify} can hold when two components touch in $\Omega$, as adjacent patches do.


\begin{corollary}[Separated images give tool-path interruptions]
  \label{cor:topology-propagation}
  Under the hypotheses of Proposition~\ref{prop:topology-transitions}(ii), suppose that distinct components have disjoint images, $\Phi(\Xs_j)\cap\Phi(\Xs_{j'})=\emptyset$ for $j\neq j'$. Then every transition is a tool-path interruption whose jump $\|{\vect{x}}(t^+)-{\vect{x}}(t^-)\|$ is at least the smallest distance between two images, and ${\vect{x}}$ has at least $N-1$ such interruptions.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
  At a transition from $\Xs_j$ to $\Xs_{j'}$, the limits ${\vect{x}}(t^-)\in\Phi(\Xs_j)$ and ${\vect{x}}(t^+)\in\Phi(\Xs_{j'})$ are at least that distance apart, which is positive because the images are disjoint and compact. Apply Proposition~\ref{prop:topology-transitions}(i).
\end{proof}

Since $\FK$ is continuous, ${\vect{q}}$ cannot be continuous where ${\vect{x}}$ is not, so every tool-path interruption is a machine interruption. By the deposition-time convention of Subsection~\ref{sec:cms-spaces}, it is also a deposition interruption, crossed by a travel move with the flow off. The converse implications fail: a connected source can still have a discontinuous traversal or geometry map, and a continuous tool path can still produce machine interruptions through a branch switch or a lift that fails near a singularity \cite{yang2020coverage,zhang2021singularity}. A connected source therefore removes only the lower bound of Proposition~\ref{prop:topology-transitions}; continuity of the later maps must be certified separately, as Appendix~\ref{sec:vsfc} does for the volumetric application.

For planar layers at spacing $h$, each with positive time share, the planar geometry map is continuous on each component and distinct layers lie at least $h$ apart, so Corollary~\ref{cor:topology-propagation}, with jumps of at least $h$, gives the stop count of Section~\ref{main:source}.
