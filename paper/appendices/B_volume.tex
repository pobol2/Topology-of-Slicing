\section{Proofs for the volumetric application}
\label{sec:vsfc}

This appendix proves the guarantees that Section~\ref{main:construction} states for the first application. Two objects are kept apart throughout: the ideal transport map $\Phi$, whose guarantees Subsections~\ref{sec:vsfc-source}--\ref{sec:vsfc-certificates} establish, and the computed centroids $\hat {\vect{x}}_i$ of Subsection~\ref{sec:vsfc-semidiscrete}, whose equal cell masses do not imply pointwise closeness to $\Phi$. All results concern the tool path and its occupation measure.

\subsection{Source and traversal}
\label{sec:vsfc-source}
\label{sec:vsfc-hilbert}

The transported path is the geometry-first chain of Definition~\ref{def:cms} with
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:vsfc-components}
  \Xs=[0,1]^3,\qquad\mu_{\Xs}=\lambda^3,
\end{equation}
where $\lambda^3$ is the uniform probability measure on the cube, the traversal ${\vect{\upxi}}$ is the three-dimensional Hilbert curve, and $\Phi$ is the optimal-transport map defined below. Any lift $\Psi_{\mathrm{g}}$ completes the tuple; the prototype stops at the tool path. The cube is connected, so Proposition~\ref{prop:topology-transitions} forces no transition; tool-path interruptions can still come from $\Sigma(\Phi)$, and machine interruptions from $B_\Psi$.

For each order $m\geq1$, split the cube into $8^m$ dyadic cells of side $2^{-m}$ and the time interval into $8^m$ blocks of length $8^{-m}$. The recursive orders must be compatible: at each level, every cell is listed once, consecutive cells share a face, and the eight children of each cell are listed consecutively at the next level. Such constructions exist \cite{haverkort2017}, though not every three-dimensional Hilbert variant has them. The Hilbert traversal ${\vect{\upxi}}$ \cite{hilbert1891,sagan1994,bader2013} has the following properties.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(H\arabic*)]
  \item \emph{Continuity and surjectivity.} ${\vect{\upxi}}$ is continuous and passes through every point of the cube.
  \item \emph{Measure preservation.} ${\vect{\upxi}}_\#\lambda^1=\lambda^3$.
  \item \emph{H\"older continuity.} $\|{\vect{\upxi}}(t)-{\vect{\upxi}}(t')\|\leq 2\sqrt6\,|t-t'|^{1/3}$.
\end{enumerate}
(H1) and (H2) are standard for compatible recursive Hilbert traversals \cite{sagan1994,bader2013}, \cite[Section~2.4]{haverkort2017}; for the nested orders assumed here, (H2) also follows because the order-$m$ maps sending each time block to its cell centre converge uniformly to ${\vect{\upxi}}$ while giving every cell a time share equal to its volume. The explicit constant in (H3) uses only face adjacency: if $8^{-(m+1)}\leq|t-t'|<8^{-m}$, the two points lie in the same or consecutive level-$m$ cells, whose union has diameter at most $\sqrt6\,2^{-m}\leq2\sqrt6\,|t-t'|^{1/3}$.


The order-$m$ curve is the sequence of cell centres ${\vect{u}}_1,\dots,{\vect{u}}_{8^m}$; face adjacency gives $\|{\vect{u}}_{i+1}-{\vect{u}}_i\|=2^{-m}$. Sending the mass of each cell to its centre moves no mass farther than half a cell diagonal, so the empirical measure $\nu^{(m)}=8^{-m}\sum_i\delta_{{\vect{u}}_i}$, with $\delta_{\vect{u}}$ the unit point mass at ${\vect{u}}$, satisfies
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:vsfc-hilbert-coverage}
  W_\infty(\nu^{(m)},\lambda^3)\leq\tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}\,2^{-m}.
\end{equation}
These finite estimates need no transport regularity.

\subsection{Transport existence and regularity}
\label{sec:vsfc-ot}
\label{app:proofs-convex-transport}

The geometry map is the solution of the Monge problem with quadratic cost, over measurable maps $\Phi'\colon[0,1]^3\to\mathbb R^3$,
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:vsfc-monge}
  \Phi=\argmin_{\Phi'\colon\Phi'_\#\lambda^3=\mu_\Omega}\int_{[0,1]^3}\|{\vect{u}}-\Phi'({\vect{u}})\|^2\,\mathrm{d}{\vect{u}}.
\end{equation}
\paragraph{Existence almost everywhere.}
Since $\lambda^3$ has a density and both measures have compact support, Brenier's theorem gives a solution, unique $\lambda^3$-almost everywhere, that is the gradient of a convex potential \cite{brenier1991,peyre2019computational}. This gives existence and uniqueness, not continuity. Without convexity, smooth densities still give a map that is smooth outside a closed singular set of measure zero \cite{dephilippis2015}. If $\Phi$ were discontinuous across a surface inside the cube, the Hilbert curve would cross it in every cell it cuts; with distinct one-sided limits at infinitely many crossings, ${\vect{x}}$ would have infinitely many discontinuities and the lift of Definition~\ref{def:cms} would not apply. This is why a finite discontinuity set is a certificate, and why convexity matters. For the torus, the L-bracket, and the complex part, only this almost-everywhere map is available; neither non-convexity nor the jumps measured in Appendix~\ref{sec:experiments} proves a singular set.

\paragraph{Continuity on the closed cube.}
Suppose $\Omega$ is compact and convex with nonempty interior, and $\mu_\Omega=\rho_\Omega\,\mathrm{d}{\vect{y}}$ on $\Omega$ with $\rho_\Omega$ bounded above and below by positive constants almost everywhere. Both densities are bounded above and below by positive constants on convex domains, so they define doubling measures, and the global regularity theory of Jhaveri and Savin supplies a H\"older-continuous representative of $\Phi$ on $[0,1]^3$ for some $0<\beta<1$ \cite[Theorem~1.1, Corollary~2.5, and Lemma~2.6]{jhaveri2022regularity}, extending Caffarelli's interior theory \cite{caffarelli1992}. Changing an almost-everywhere representative preserves the pushforward, and smooth or uniformly convex boundaries are not required. This covers uniform measures on full-dimensional convex boxes, cylinders, spheres, and convex polyhedra. It supplies no numerical value of the H\"older constant, and convexity alone is insufficient when the target density vanishes, is unbounded, or lives on a lower-dimensional set.

\subsection{Path certificates}
\label{sec:vsfc-certificates}
\label{sec:vsfc-construction}

Convex regularity gives the geometry stage the certificate
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:vsfc-holder}
  \|\Phi({\vect{u}})-\Phi({\vect{u}}')\|\leq C_\Phi\|{\vect{u}}-{\vect{u}}'\|^\beta
  \qquad\text{for all }{\vect{u}},{\vect{u}}'\in[0,1]^3,
\end{equation}
with some $\beta\in(0,1]$ and finite but unknown $C_\Phi$. For a non-convex part it remains a hypothesis. An implementation produces waypoints $\hat {\vect{x}}_i$ with error $\varepsilon=\max_i\|\hat {\vect{x}}_i-\Phi({\vect{u}}_i)\|$.

\begin{theorem}[Ideal-path and finite-waypoint guarantees]
  \label{thm:vsfc-guarantees}
  \begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
    \item \emph{Conditional bounds.} Under \eqref{eq:vsfc-holder}, the ideal path ${\vect{x}}=\Phi\circ{\vect{\upxi}}$ is continuous, $(\beta/3)$-H\"older, and has occupation measure ${\vect{x}}_\#\lambda^1=\mu_\Omega$. Waypoints within $\varepsilon$ of $\Phi({\vect{u}}_i)$ satisfy
          \begin{equation}
            \label{eq:vsfc-bounds}
            W_\infty\Bigl(8^{-m}\textstyle\sum_i\delta_{\hat {\vect{x}}_i},\,\mu_\Omega\Bigr)
            \leq C_\Phi\bigl(\tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}\bigr)^{\beta}2^{-\beta m}+\varepsilon,
            \qquad
            \|\hat {\vect{x}}_{i+1}-\hat {\vect{x}}_i\|\leq C_\Phi2^{-\beta m}+2\varepsilon.
          \end{equation}
          The first bound also bounds the target covering radius.
    \item \emph{Convex setting.} If $\Omega$ is compact and convex with nonempty interior and its target density is bounded above and below by positive constants, then \eqref{eq:vsfc-holder} holds for some $\beta\in(0,1]$ and finite $C_\Phi$. Thus (i) applies: the ideal path has no tool-path interruption and exactly the prescribed occupation measure.
  \end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
  For (i), (H3) and Proposition~\ref{prop:composition}(ii) give H\"older continuity, and (H2) gives ${\vect{x}}_\#\lambda^1=\Phi{}_\#\lambda^3=\mu_\Omega$. Apply Proposition~\ref{prop:composition}(i) to $\nu^{(m)}$ and $\Phi$, using \eqref{eq:vsfc-hilbert-coverage}; moving each waypoint by at most $\varepsilon$ adds $\varepsilon$. Corollary~\ref{cor:cms-covering-radius} supplies the covering-radius bound. Face adjacency gives $\|{\vect{u}}_{i+1}-{\vect{u}}_i\|=2^{-m}$, so \eqref{eq:vsfc-holder} bounds the ideal step by $C_\Phi2^{-\beta m}$, and the triangle inequality adds $2\varepsilon$. For (ii), the closed-domain regularity of Subsection~\ref{app:proofs-convex-transport} supplies \eqref{eq:vsfc-holder}.
\end{proof}
A lift with Lipschitz constant $L_\Psi$ on a region containing the waypoints bounds the joint steps by $L_\Psi(C_\Phi2^{-\beta m}+2\varepsilon)$.

The finite bounds are conditional on $\varepsilon$, which is not certified for the computed path: the semi-discrete computation below certifies equal cell mass and the measured bound $d^{(m)}$ instead, and neither bounds $\varepsilon$.

\subsection{Computed-path guarantees}
\label{sec:vsfc-semidiscrete}

\paragraph{Power cells and equal-mass optimality.}
Let $\mu_\Omega=\rho_\Omega\,\mathrm{d}{\vect{y}}$ with $\rho_\Omega>0$ integrable on $\Omega$, let ${\vect{u}}_1,\dots,{\vect{u}}_n$ be the distinct sites of the order-$m$ curve, and let $\nu^{(m)}=n^{-1}\sum_i\delta_{{\vect{u}}_i}$. For weights ${\vect{\uppsi}}=(\psi_1,\dots,\psi_n)\in\mathbb R^n$, the \emph{Laguerre cell} of site ${\vect{u}}_i$ in a region $A\subseteq\mathbb R^3$ is the part of $A$ that is power-nearest to it,
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:vsfc-laguerre}
  L_i(A;{\vect{\uppsi}})=\bigl\{{\vect{y}}\in A:\ \|{\vect{y}}-{\vect{u}}_i\|^2-\psi_i\leq\|{\vect{y}}-{\vect{u}}_{i'}\|^2-\psi_{i'}\ \text{for all }i'\bigr\}.
\end{equation}
The uncut cell $L_i(\mathbb R^3;{\vect{\uppsi}})$ is a convex polyhedron, an intersection of half-spaces, and the cell of the part is $L_i=L_i(\Omega;{\vect{\uppsi}})=L_i(\mathbb R^3;{\vect{\uppsi}})\cap\Omega$. Two cells meet only where two power distances are equal, on a plane, so the cells tile $\Omega$ up to a null set. Weights satisfying \eqref{eq:main-equal-mass} make the map ${\vect{y}}\mapsto {\vect{u}}_i$ on $L_i$ the optimal transport from $\mu_\Omega$ to $\nu^{(m)}$ \cite{aurenhammer1998minkowski,merigot2011multiscale,levy2015numerical}. The waypoints are the centroids $\hat {\vect{x}}_i$ of \eqref{eq:main-centroid}, and $d^{(m)}=\max_i\operatorname{diam}L_i$.

\begin{proposition}[What equal cell mass controls]
  \label{prop:sd-mass}
  Let ${\vect{\uppsi}}$ satisfy \eqref{eq:main-equal-mass} and $\hat\nu^{(m)}=n^{-1}\sum_i\delta_{\hat {\vect{x}}_i}$.
  \begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
    \item \emph{Distribution.} $W_\infty(\hat\nu^{(m)},\mu_\Omega)\leq d^{(m)}$.
    \item \emph{Order.} $\hat {\vect{x}}_i\in L_i(\mathbb R^3;{\vect{\uppsi}})$, so $\hat {\vect{x}}_i$ is power-nearest to its own site ${\vect{u}}_i$.
    \item \emph{Waypoint density.} For every Borel set $E$, with $E^{+}=\{{\vect{y}}:\dist({\vect{y}},E)\leq d^{(m)}\}$ and $E^{-}=\{{\vect{y}}:\dist({\vect{y}},\mathbb R^3\setminus E)>d^{(m)}\}$,
          \[
            \mu_\Omega(E^{-})\leq\hat\nu^{(m)}(E)\leq\mu_\Omega(E^{+}),\qquad
            \bigl|\hat\nu^{(m)}(E)-\mu_\Omega(E)\bigr|\leq\mu_\Omega(E^{+}\setminus E^{-}).
          \]
    \item \emph{Containment.} If $\Omega$ is convex, every $\hat {\vect{x}}_i$ and every segment $[\hat {\vect{x}}_i,\hat {\vect{x}}_{i+1}]$ lies in $\Omega$.
    \item \emph{Consistency.} If $\Omega=[0,1]^3$ and $\mu_\Omega=\lambda^3$, ${\vect{\uppsi}}=0$ solves \eqref{eq:main-equal-mass}, each $L_i$ is the Hilbert cell of ${\vect{u}}_i$, and $\hat {\vect{x}}_i={\vect{u}}_i$.
  \end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
  $\hat {\vect{x}}_i$ is the mean of the probability measure $n\,\mu_\Omega|_{L_i}$, so it lies in the closed convex hull of $L_i$. For ${\vect{y}}\in L_i$, the distance from ${\vect{y}}$ to a point of that hull is at most the largest distance from ${\vect{y}}$ to a point of $\overline{L_i}$, hence at most $\operatorname{diam}L_i\leq d^{(m)}$. (i) The coupling that sends each cell's mass to its centroid has first marginal $\mu_\Omega$, because the cells tile $\Omega$ up to a null set, and second marginal $\sum_i\mu_\Omega(L_i)\delta_{\hat {\vect{x}}_i}=\hat\nu^{(m)}$ by \eqref{eq:main-equal-mass}; it moves no mass farther than $d^{(m)}$. (ii) $L_i\subseteq L_i(\mathbb R^3;{\vect{\uppsi}})$, which is closed and convex, so it contains the hull of $L_i$ and hence $\hat {\vect{x}}_i$. (iii) If $\hat {\vect{x}}_i\in E$, every point of $L_i$ is within $d^{(m)}$ of $\hat {\vect{x}}_i$, so $L_i\subseteq E^{+}$; summing over these disjoint cells gives $\hat\nu^{(m)}(E)\leq\mu_\Omega(E^{+})$. If $L_i$ meets $E^{-}$ at some ${\vect{y}}$, then $\|\hat {\vect{x}}_i-{\vect{y}}\|\leq d^{(m)}<\dist({\vect{y}},\mathbb R^3\setminus E)$, so $\hat {\vect{x}}_i\in E$; the cells meeting $E^{-}$ cover it, which gives $\mu_\Omega(E^{-})\leq\hat\nu^{(m)}(E)$. Since $E^{-}\subseteq E\subseteq E^{+}$, $\mu_\Omega(E)$ lies in the same interval, and the difference is at most its length. (iv) $L_i=L_i(\mathbb R^3;{\vect{\uppsi}})\cap\Omega$ is convex and contains $\hat {\vect{x}}_i$; a convex $\Omega$ contains the segment between two of its points. (v) With ${\vect{\uppsi}}=0$, the power cells are the Voronoi cells of the cube grid, that is, the Hilbert cells, each of volume $1/n$ and centroid ${\vect{u}}_i$.
\end{proof}
A computed solution meets \eqref{eq:main-equal-mass} only to a relative tolerance, and (i) and (iii) then hold up to that tolerance. Item (iii) makes the waypoint share of a region accurate only when the region is thick compared with $d^{(m)}$. Nothing in the proposition bounds $d^{(m)}$ or keeps $\hat {\vect{x}}_i$ in a non-convex $\Omega$: a clipped cell can be non-convex or disconnected, its centroid can lie outside, and its diameter can stay large as $m$ grows. Nor does it bound $\varepsilon=\max_i\|\hat {\vect{x}}_i-\Phi({\vect{u}}_i)\|$: on convex parts quantitative stability results give $L^2$ rates \cite{merigot2020quantitative}, but no pointwise rate is established here.

\paragraph{Convex-hull gap.}
\label{sec:vsfc-hull-gap}
For a nonempty compact set $\Omega\subset\mathbb R^3$, the part or a piece $\Omega_j$ of it, define its \emph{convex-hull gap} by
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:vsfc-hull-gap}
  \gamma(\Omega)=\sup_{{\vect{y}}\in\operatorname{conv}(\Omega)}\dist({\vect{y}},\Omega).
\end{equation}
Near-convexity means that $\gamma(\Omega)$ is small at the geometric scale of interest.

\begin{proposition}[Convex-hull gap bounds centroid-path excursions]
  \label{prop:sd-hull-gap}
  Let $\Omega\subset\mathbb R^3$ be nonempty and compact, let $\mu_\Omega$ be a finite Borel measure on $\Omega$, and let $L_1,\ldots,L_n\subseteq\Omega$ be measurable cells with $\mu_\Omega(L_i)>0$. Define
  \[
    \hat {\vect{x}}_i=\frac{\int_{L_i}{\vect{y}}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_\Omega({\vect{y}})}{\mu_\Omega(L_i)}.
  \]
  \begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
    \item Every $\hat {\vect{x}}_i$ and every straight segment $[\hat {\vect{x}}_i,\hat {\vect{x}}_{i'}]$ lie in $\operatorname{conv}(\Omega)$ and have distance at most $\gamma(\Omega)$ from $\Omega$. In particular, for any polygonal path $\hat{\vect{x}}$ whose vertices are chosen from these centroids,
          \begin{equation}
            \label{eq:vsfc-hull-excursion}
            \sup_{t\in[0,1]}\dist\bigl(\hat{\vect{x}}(t),\Omega\bigr)\leq\gamma(\Omega).
          \end{equation}
          Moreover, $\gamma(\Omega)=0$ if and only if $\Omega$ is convex, in which case containment in $\Omega$ is exact.
    \item \emph{Partitioned parts.} Let $\Omega=\bigcup_{j=1}^N\Omega_j$ be a union of compact pieces with $\mu_\Omega(\Omega_j\cap\Omega_{j'})=0$ for $j\neq j'$ and positive target-mass shares $w_j=\mu_\Omega(\Omega_j)$. Run the semi-discrete algorithm independently on each piece, with $n_j$ Hilbert sites and the conditional target measure $\mu_\Omega(\,\cdot\mid\Omega_j)=w_j^{-1}\mu_\Omega|_{\Omega_j}$, and give piece $j$ deposition-time share $w_j$. Each local cell of an equal-mass solve then has global mass and waypoint time share $w_j/n_j$, and the computed path $\hat{\vect{x}}_j$ through the centroids of piece $j$ in Hilbert order satisfies $\sup_{t\in[0,1]}\dist\bigl(\hat{\vect{x}}_j(t),\Omega_j\bigr)\leq\gamma(\Omega_j)$, staying in $\Omega_j$ if $\Omega_j$ is convex.
  \end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
  (i) The convex hull $\operatorname{conv}(\Omega)$ of a compact set in finite-dimensional space is compact, hence closed and convex. Each $\hat {\vect{x}}_i$ is the mean of the normalised measure $\mu_\Omega(\,\cdot\mid L_i)$, whose support lies in $\Omega$. Such a mean belongs to its closed convex hull: otherwise a separating hyperplane would put the mean strictly beyond all points of its support, contradicting the defining integral. Thus $\hat {\vect{x}}_i\in\operatorname{conv}(\Omega)$. Convexity of $\operatorname{conv}(\Omega)$ contains every segment $[\hat {\vect{x}}_i,\hat {\vect{x}}_{i'}]$ and therefore $\hat{\vect{x}}$. Equation~\eqref{eq:vsfc-hull-gap} bounds the distance to $\Omega$ of every point in $\operatorname{conv}(\Omega)$, proving \eqref{eq:vsfc-hull-excursion}. Finally, convexity gives $\operatorname{conv}(\Omega)=\Omega$ and $\gamma(\Omega)=0$. Conversely, if $\gamma(\Omega)=0$, every point of $\operatorname{conv}(\Omega)$ has zero distance to the closed set $\Omega$, so $\operatorname{conv}(\Omega)=\Omega$. (ii) Each local cell $L_{ji}$ has conditional mass $\mu_\Omega(L_{ji}\mid\Omega_j)=1/n_j$, and $\mu_\Omega=\sum_jw_j\,\mu_\Omega(\,\cdot\mid\Omega_j)$ gives $\mu_\Omega(L_{ji})=w_j/n_j$. Apply (i) to each local solve, with $\Omega_j$ in place of $\Omega$ and $\mu_\Omega(\,\cdot\mid\Omega_j)$ in place of $\mu_\Omega$.
\end{proof}
The bound does not require equal cell masses, convex cells, a particular visiting order, or regularity of the transport map, and it bounds excursion depth only. The ratio of the volumes of $\Omega$ and $\operatorname{conv}(\Omega)$ cannot replace $\gamma(\Omega)$, because a narrow indentation may have little missing volume and substantial depth.
